Z reference · no homing needed

More info

Klipper will not move Z until it thinks Z is homed. This tells it "Z is the number above, right here", with no motion. The number is a label you choose, not a measured height. It clears the floor (the old one belonged to the old label) and sets a ceiling above it. Home X and Y first. Do this again after any firmware restart.

not set
More info

Z cannot move down until a floor exists, then never below it. Start with "Floor = Z − this", jog down to just above the surface, then "Floor = current Z" to lock it tight. Jobs obey it too.

Zero and scale

Two steps, both save by themselves. 1) Cell empty: Tare. 2) Press a known mass DOWN on the cell, type its grams, Calibrate scale. Do this once; it is remembered across restarts. Every Z home and check also tares by itself.

Where is the load cell
More info

Jog the swab tip over the middle of the cell and capture. "Go to" moves X and Y only, at whatever Z you are at. The start Z only needs to be a few mm above the cell: the probe finds the real height itself by stepping down until the force reads the target.

Probe test

More info

Order: rise to the start Z if below it, move over the cell at that height, settle onto the start Z, tare, then step down at the descent speed. Z never moves at travel speed. Each step waits for the move to finish and a force reading, so the average speed is a little below the setting, most visibly with small steps.

Load cell to glass offset

More info

The load cell sits above the glass. A job finds the swab's contact height on the cell, then adds this offset to get the scribe height on the slide. Use the same swab for both touches.

  1. Contact on the load cell
    Cell contact Znot set

    Probe on the Load cell tab, or jog the swab down onto the cell by hand until it just reads force.

  2. Contact on the glass
    Glass contact Znot set
    More info

    Jog over the slide, lower in 0.05 to 0.1 mm steps until the swab just touches the glass, then capture. Stay above the Z floor.

  3. Offset
    Glass − cell–Locked value–

    Scribe Z = cell contact Z + offset. The offset is normally negative because the glass is lower than the cell.

Gripper servo

First run: no swab in the jaws, jaws clear of everything. The very first command after power-up jumps (the horn's resting spot is unknown), so start it near the middle of the window. Every move after that ramps at the chosen speed and cannot leave the window. Widen the window only in small steps.

        Reference substrate · glass, red dot and G-code

        One substrate whose toolpath you already have as G-code. Every scan then compares each new substrate's glass to this one (the red dot says which way round it is) and moves the G-code by the same turn and shift. Download the moved G-code from each row of the substrate table.

        1. Photo of the reference substrate, from straight above (phone is fine; JPEG or PNG, or HEIC in Safari):
        2. Click its 4 glass corners in the photo (any order). The red dot corner is found by itself; change it below if it is wrong.
        3. Where that glass sat on the printer when the G-code was made.
        4. Its toolpath: the bridge sketch of this substrate (.json from the sketcher, drawn over the whole glass with 0, 0 at the photo's top-left glass corner), or a G-code file.

        With a sketch, each scanned substrate gets these bridges placed on its glass (turned and shifted like the glass) and runs with Scrub / Routine 1 or downloads as G-code. The sketch always wins: a G-code file is only used (moved the same way) when no sketch is loaded. A substrate with its own sketch keeps its own.

        In the photo: gold = glass corners, red ring = red dot corner, pink = sketch bridges, green = electrode array (found by itself; click "electrode array corners" to fix it), orange = the G-code's cutting moves, grey = travel. New substrates' glass is estimated from their array the same way as on this one; set a substrate's glass by hand in the sketcher if needed. Needs the camera points.

        Camera points · camera to printer X/Y

        These tell the GUI where the printer's X and Y are in the camera photo, so it can turn each scanned substrate into printer millimetres. You need them once (again only if the camera moves). Each point is a small mark on the plate that you show the camera and the swab tip:

        1. Pick a mark the camera can see: a screw head, a hole, a pen dot on the plate (at about substrate height).
        2. Click it in the photo below. An orange cross shows where you clicked.
        3. Jog the swab tip (Manual test & calibration) until it sits exactly over that same mark.
        4. Press Pair. That saves "this pixel is this printer X, Y".
        5. Repeat for 3 or 4 more marks spread out over the plate. Two is the minimum; a fifth checks the others.

        If the tip hides the mark, click the mark first, then jog. Press Refresh photo if the photo is old.

        Checks

        A substrate whose measured array differs from its pattern by more than the tolerance, or is turned more than the largest turn, is flagged and will not run unless you confirm it. Arrays must sit within 45° of the nominal orientation for the top-left corner to be found.
